## 1. Bottom line

The core contribution survives, but three sentences in the current framing must be rewritten, and two planned analyses are at risk as designed.

No paper, preprint, workshop submission, thesis or blog post fits a sequential latent learner model (BKT, HMM, dynamic IRT, Kalman or state-space IRT, deep KT) to an LLM's benchmark responses across training checkpoints, validates it by predicting held-out later checkpoints at item level, reports per-skill mastery trajectories with uncertainty, or tests item-parameter drift within one model's checkpoints. Checked across roughly 60 arXiv queries, 53 forward citations of the five closest papers plus 27 second-ring citations, 6,237 OpenReview submissions (ICLR 2025/2026, NeurIPS 2024/2025, NeurIPS 2026 TAE workshop, ICML 2026), and web search. ICLR 2027 and NeurIPS 2026 main-track submissions are not public, so that surface cannot be closed today.

What must change:

1. **"No one has fit IRT to LLM item responses across pretraining checkpoints" is false.** Item Response Scaling Laws (arXiv 2606.07616) fits 1PL/2PL and Beta-IRT to 6,612 real pretraining checkpoints × 37,682 items, a subsample of AI2's DataDecide release. What it does not have: any sequential state (each checkpoint's ability is estimated independently, then a static regression through the cloud), any test of item invariance (item parameters calibrated once and frozen, so drift is structurally invisible), any posterior over ability, any per-skill decomposition, or held-out-later-checkpoint validation. Its ability is `θ ≈ a·log(FLOP) + b`, a deterministic function of compute, so ability cannot decrease and forgetting is unrepresentable.
2. **The per-item difficulty-drift test, as framed, cannot detect anything.** Applying the detectability bound in arXiv 2606.14123 (Prop. 2, `δ_min ≥ z_α √(8/n_min)`) to our data density gives a minimum detectable per-checkpoint drift of 2.26 logits per item pooled over six models (1.96 × √(8/6)), larger than the plausible range of item difficulty. It must be re-posed at bank level (test a drift variance Q > 0 with a Kalman smoother) or in coarse bins (early/mid/late thirds, δ_min ≈ 0.4), and should target discrimination, where prior evidence says drift lives.
3. **MMLU may contain very little separable per-subject mastery to trace.** A 2PL audit of all 14,042 MMLU items × 1,000 models (arXiv 2609.09372, §4.3 and App. C) finds STEM and non-STEM abilities correlate at ρ = 0.965 (disattenuated subscale check 0.977). The risk to Design decision 3 is not label noise; it is that 57 mastery trajectories may all move together. If they separate across checkpoints where they do not separate across final models, that is a stronger paper than the one planned. Measure it early.

Everything else is either support (with numbers) or a cheap, concrete fix. Section 6 lists the fixes in the order to do them.

## 2. The three most important things we did not already know

**The observation grid is much bigger than Fluid Benchmarking, it is public, and someone has already fit static IRT to it.** `allenai/DataDecide-eval-instances` (ODC-BY) holds ~150k checkpoints and ~500M instance-level binary evaluations with MMLU pre-split into all 57 subjects, roughly 40× our Phase 1 data, at 4M to 1B parameters (so MMLU stays near chance; ARC-Easy, HellaSwag, PIQA are the skills it can support). It ships as 25 per-mix tarballs totalling 122.9 GB, about 5 GB each. The Implicit Curriculum repository (`KaiserWhoLearns/ElementalTask`, MIT) ships 6,047 per-(model, checkpoint, task) result files with per-item correct flags for Amber, OLMo-2 1B/7B and Pythia-6.9B on 91 tasks with an elemental/composite prerequisite structure: three of our own learners on a second, skill-decomposed suite, and nobody has modelled it. IRSL is the only paper to have fit any psychometric model to a checkpoint grid, and it is static per checkpoint. The framing should say the data existed and nobody fitted a learner model to it.

**Two planned measurements are underpowered or at risk as designed, and both are fixable before any model is fit.** (a) Per-item drift: δ_min = 2.26 logits (above). (b) Per-subject separability: ρ = 0.965 (above). A third measurement, the five-subject forgetting list, is decidable now: arXiv 2602.10144 §3.2 shows the variance of a paired accuracy drop is p_flip / N, so a 0.08 drop on a 150-item subject is about 3σ at a 10% flip rate and only about 1.7σ at 35%. Computing the observed flip rate on our own data is an afternoon's work and determines whether there is a forgetting signal to trace at all. Also: standard errors on any pooled item-level result should be clustered by subject (2609.09372 §4.3 finds CR2 clustering inflates SEs by a median 1.65×), and the item-mean bar of 0.360 is a small margin to defend with anticonservative intervals.

**The right primary model comes from political science, and the KT tooling we planned already does what we need.** Martin and Quinn's dynamic IRT as implemented in `emIRT::dynIRT` (CRAN 0.0.15, published 2025-09-23, GPL ≥ 3, variational EM with a Kalman filter per unit) is the only candidate that is simultaneously batched per period, shared-item-bank with per-item difficulty and discrimination, cheap, and built for few units over many periods (9 justices × 47 terms). Its one gap, irregular spacing, is a two-line change at `src/getX_dynIRT.cpp` L69 and L96 (`Ot(i,t) = omega2(i,0) + ...` becomes `omega2(i,0) * gap(t) + ...`). pyBKT (MIT, pushed 2026-08-05) already implements batched observations per time step with one transition per column through its internal Data format (`EM_fit.py` L197 to 205: likelihood is a product over all subparts in a column, then one transition per column), so the BKT branch needs a data-packing shim and no algorithm change. VTIRT, the brief's first-named candidate, is disqualified: one item per time step by construction (Eq. 4), experiments on 2,500 to 20,000 learners, and no licence on the repository.

## 9b. Corrections after independent review (2026-09-30, evening)

A second, independent review of Workbook 1 (`docs/workbook/Workbook1-review-2026-09-30.md`) found errors in this document's method claims. Each was re-checked against the primary source and is corrected here rather than silently rewritten above.

- **emIRT::dynIRT is not a drop-in shared-item-bank model.** The CRAN manual defines `bill.session` as "a (J x 1) matrix of integers indicating the session each bill occurred in": each item belongs to exactly one period, and item parameters α, β are indexed per item, not shared across periods. Repeating each question as a separate item per checkpoint does not tie the parameters. The "two-line patch" in Section 2 and Section 3.2 addresses uneven spacing only; parameter tying across checkpoints needs a custom model. The manual also states that the variational variances "are known to be far too small and generally unusable"; uncertainty must come from a parametric bootstrap or an MCMC refit.
- **Martin and Quinn (2002)** analysed 29 justices (at most nine per term) over 47 terms with cases that change every term, and estimated by MCMC with forward-filtering backward-sampling. It is structural precedent for a random-walk ability, not a shared-item-bank Kalman filter.
- **pyBKT batching.** The internal Data format allows several observations per time step, but `EM_fit.py` multiplies raw emission probabilities across subparts before normalising; for a batch of 1,534 items that product underflows to zero in double precision. A log-space forward-backward is required. This is a code-reading finding, not an executed test.
- **BKT and the AUC bar.** With one guess/slip pair per skill, every item in a skill receives the same probability, so within-skill AUC is 0.50 by construction. BKT can only compete on AUC with per-item emissions (pyBKT's multigs), which reintroduces the few-learner estimation problem.
- **T-SKIRT gains.** The +2.8 / +2.4 figures are for the full temporal + structured multidimensional model; the temporal-only ablation gains +2.19 accuracy / +1.65 AUC. The closed form is derived for a probit (normal-ogive) 2PO model, so Fluid's logistic 2PL parameters need conversion or refitting.
- **IRSL** estimates each checkpoint's ability independently and then fits a scaling law through them; the fitted curve is monotone in compute, but the per-checkpoint estimates are not constrained to be. "Ability cannot decrease" applies to the fitted law, not the estimates.
- **Implicit Curriculum** does predict held-out *task* trajectories from function vectors. "No held-out prediction" was wrong; the remaining distinction is item-level forecasting of later checkpoints within a run.
- **MMLU audit (2609.09372)** concludes STEM and non-STEM abilities are *distinguishable* (ρ = 0.965 < 1, two-dimensional model fits held-out responses better). It motivates testing subject separability; it is not evidence that subjects are indistinguishable.
- **Detectability bound.** Prop. 2's unequal-bin simplification `4/n1 + 4/n2 ≥ 8/n_min` is false in general (n1 = 1, n2 = 100 gives 4.04 < 8); the equal-bin case we used is unaffected. More importantly, the bound is a significance threshold under independence assumptions that our correlated six-model panel may violate. Treat δ_min = 2.26 as a first-order estimate and run a project-specific simulation-based power analysis.
- **ATLAS** primarily calibrates 3PL banks (2PL as comparison) and publishes no MMLU bank, so it is not a direct initialisation for all our items.
- **Fluid Benchmarking** reports 467 checkpoints (61 for K2) in its Appendix C; the release we inspected contains 452 (46 for K2). Report both with provenance.
- **Novelty wording.** Replace "the conjunction does not exist" with: in the sources reviewed through 2026-09-30, we did not identify a method that jointly models a stochastic per-skill latent state across an LLM's pretraining checkpoints, treats each checkpoint's responses as a batch, and evaluates item-level predictions on later held-out checkpoints with calibrated uncertainty.
