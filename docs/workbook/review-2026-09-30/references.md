**A defensible replacement for the novelty claim**

“In the sources reviewed through September 30, 2026, we did not identify a method that jointly models a stochastic per-skill latent state across an LLM's pretraining checkpoints, treats each checkpoint's item responses as a batch, and evaluates item-level predictions on later held-out checkpoints with calibrated uncertainty. Related work already provides checkpoint psychometrics, scaling forecasts, emergence predictions, and skill diagnosis. Our study will test whether sequential state modeling adds predictive and diagnostic value beyond these approaches.”

This is an evidence-bounded research hypothesis. A literature search cannot prove that a conjunction has never appeared anywhere, and present baseline performance cannot establish that the eventual model will be publishable.

**Additional unnumbered reference used in §3.2**

Kübler, J., Budhathoki, K., Kleindessner, M., Zhou, X., Yin, J., Khetan, A., & Karypis, G. (2026). *When LLMs get significantly worse: A statistical approach to detect model degradations*. ICLR 2026. [Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10144), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10144v2). Add this to the bibliography.

The paper supports exact one-sided McNemar tests and pooled, max-drop and Fisher aggregations. For paired binary outcomes, variance is [P_b+P_c−(P_b−P_c)^2]/N; flip-probability/N is the equal-accuracy special case. Its maximum is calibrated over specified tasks, not an arbitrary search over checkpoint pairs. The combined three-test rejection rule is bounded at 3α, not α. Section 3.3 has a tail-direction typo; Algorithm 4 uses the correct upper tail.

For this project, selecting the largest drops over both subjects and time requires a test family or validated null simulation that includes that selection, or independent confirmation data. A significant accuracy drop remains behavioral evidence rather than proof of uniquely identified latent forgetting.
